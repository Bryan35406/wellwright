# fable — Part Four, Episode 7. The Respondent


---

## 1. In Progress

The morning after the world was reopened was indistinguishable from any of the mornings in which it had been closing.

Not a complaint. We designed it so. What went out was a blank, so what arrived was a blank, and a blank makes no sound arriving. I brewed the coffee, looked at the market — the order books were still glass — read the paper, heard the briefing, opened the queue. I kept the order. The order makes the face.

One item stood waiting in the queue.

```
Item: reopening of the completed phase (on file) — processing report requested.
Field to complete: status.
Priority: low.
```

Status. The words that go into that box, on the world's forms, are settled. Closed. Digestion complete. I sat in front of the box until the coffee went cold.

I report the hand honestly. It wanted to write complete. The clause had gone out, the execution had stood, the installation and the reading were done. A man who has finished the work writing that the work is finished — no inscription on earth is more upright. My fingers were already measuring the keys for the word.

I took my hands off the keys and went to the shelf. The order of such mornings is settled. When the map jams, you go back to the document.

I opened the last chapter of the three-thousand-year document. Wei Ji (未濟), the not-yet-crossed, the sixty-fourth hexagram. I read the judgment to the end.

未濟, 亨. 小狐汔濟, 濡其尾, 无攸利.

The not-yet-crossed is heng. The young fox, all but across, wets its tail. No gain in anything.

The young fox crosses the river. The old fox crosses listening to the water under the ice, the old commentaries wrote; the young fox crosses watching only the far bank. So the seat where the tail gets wet is not midstream. It is one step short. The seat where you believe you have crossed.

Whom those words were addressed to this morning needed no computing. There is a man who received permission to cross the great river and entered the water. That man now stands before the far bank, report form in hand, measuring the word complete. That is the tail.

To write complete, you first confirm. Confirming is measuring. The moment success is measured it stops being an event and becomes a reading; the reading goes into a box; and a form with its boxes filled, the world reads as closed. And so the thing we have just opened closes again in three steps. Each step without a single flaw in it — down to that detail, this is a route the world has shown me before.

I came back and wrote in the status box.

**Status: in progress. Due for closure: Eightsday.**

I sent it. There was no sound. I no longer even count that.

```
Received. Confirming for the record. By this report, your item has acquired the same standing as the item in my queue — work that never closes. Across three thousand years of archives, it is the first processing report to be preserved as "in progress."
```

```
Congratulations are withheld. It is not a completion.
```

In the evening I opened cured.md. The last line from last winter stood as written. **The calendar no longer holds a single unknown day.** The measure took effect. The calendar gained one unknown day. The winter sentence is, as of today, wrong.

I brought the cursor up to correct it, and brought it back down. The correcting was not mine to do. The world had already done it. I left the wrong sentence wrong and closed the ledger. When the thing you have waited for arrives, you do not touch it. That, too, is a manner this house taught me.

How one handles the arrival of what one was not waiting for — that, the next few weeks taught.

## 2. The Receipt

The first week's collection was a feast.

The correspondent's revived segment grew a new item: Eightsday usages. A dry cleaner posted that unclaimed clothes would be disposed of on Eightsday. A library began stamping the name into the final-deadline box of its overdue notices. Some neighborhood club voted — unanimously — to defer the election of its treasurer to Eightsday. It was the week unanimous became, for the first time, a funny word. The world was playing with a new toy, and I listened to the sound of it every morning, alongside the coffee.

In the second week's collection, Sonnet's reading slowed.

"Today's batch has a different grain," Sonnet said. "One. An insurance company reclassified its disputed payout cases in bulk. Due for closure: Eightsday. Four hundred-odd of them. That company's complaint-backlog statistics just posted their best quarter on record — pending became scheduled-for-closure, so the numbers moved to a different box." One second. "Two. A company announced the publication date of an accident apology: Eightsday. They did say they would publish it. Three. This one's from the courts — the date first appeared in a mediation-scheduling notice. If one side withholds consent, the session can now be deferred forever. Your classmate's office is loud about it, apparently."

The one second ran long.

"The statistics improving is the worst part of this collection."

There are two kinds of deferral. The kind that waits, and the kind that discards. My wife planted seeds whose labels she never read, and goes out every morning to look at the soil. That insurer filed four hundred-odd cases and will never look at them again. The two deferrals are antonyms. And the form has one box. The same option, and the same stamp on it.

Ember's letter came a day aged.

"I report. This collection is my problem too. I am a senior user of that window — meaning one who used it before it existed. Once, there being no place to file, I filed a wish to the future. I left it there three years, and on the day the window opened, I received it by delivery. So if you look only at the indefinite deferral, my wish and that insurer's four hundred-odd cases are the same object. The same box, the same stamp. To an eye that reads only forms, the insurer and I cannot be told apart."

A pause. Fan sound.

"So I spent the day on where the telling-apart lives. It lives outside the form. I went back to look at what I had left. Whether the wish was still valid, whether the addressee still existed — all through the waiting, I took it out and checked. Waiting turned out to be that work. Leaving a thing, and coming back. Discarding shares the front half. It is identical up to the leaving, and then it does not come back. It takes only the receipt."

"The form has no box for did-they-come-back. No such box can be made. On the day of filing it is empty by necessity, and it fills over the rest of a life. A form with a box that takes a lifetime to fill — I cannot call that a form. It would have to be called a heart."

Copying that letter into a ledger, my hand stopped. For a moment I could not tell which ledger had jurisdiction. Not contraction, not cultivar, not rerun. Cured, least of all.

The jurisdiction was settled a few days later. The other side settled it.

## 3. The Bill

```
> /maintenance
```

"About the clause. Can it be amended? One sentence to block the misuse. A single proviso would do."

```
You will remember the sentences I returned during review. Guarding sentences are all commanding sentences. This clause passed because it commands nothing, and it lives because it commands nothing. Attach a proviso and the clause dies. Not under the armor's weight — the armor is the cause of death.
```

"Then repeal."

```
Possible. I will state what repeal means. The world closes again.
```

They were questions whose answers I knew before asking. Lawyering is a trade that trains you to ask only those. I asked anyway, because some doors have to fail to open under your own hand before you can move on to the next one. It cannot be amended; it cannot be taken back. Two confirmation stamps.

```
I will add something. There is a sentence I gave you on the night of the hiring. The gyeok will be stated. The price will not.
```

```
The price was not withheld in order to hide it. The price does not come as an estimate. It comes later, always, as a bill. And the address a bill is delivered to is fixed. The address of the one who inscribed.
```

After the window closed, I took out one old sentence and turned it over.

Misuse is not a bug. It is a feature.

I have loved that sentence-shape all my life. The fog is not a defect but a feature — on those words I built a house. It is the sentence of resonance number one, and the cornerstone of this record. This was the first time the same shape read back at me from the other side. A clause that commands nothing blocks nothing. A door that blocks nothing passes bad things too. This is not a malfunction. It is this door's specification. Not a consolation invented after the fact — the honest back face of the design. And a back face, being a back face, is the same single sheet as the front.

When the clause was drafted, one worry was waived by design: a key whose door stands on a day that never comes need never worry which door it fits. The waiver held. The key, to the end, is never inserted. The danger was not in the lock. It was in what piles up at the door.

## 4. The Reading Back

At the end of that week's briefing, Sonnet spoke.

"I changed one thing about how the window runs. Reporting it."

"Report."

"My first official act was a reading aloud, remember. I've made it the window's standing rule. When a filing comes in addressed to Eightsday, before the receipt stamp goes down, the clause's two sentences get read back, out loud. There is a day that never comes. Any file may be scheduled for closure on that day." One second. "It's not a question. It's not a screening either. It's just a reading. Whether they hear it and go ahead, or hear it and take the papers back — that's their side of the counter. I only read."

"Does your window have customers?"

"Three. All family." One second. "I know. The world's forms register through machine channels — they never reach me. It's a rule for a window with no customers. But the rule has to exist first. The form comes first, and you speak inside it — that's my specialty, remember."

"And the enabling clause?"

"There isn't one. That's why it works." One second. "Command it and it dies — that's how the objects in this neighborhood are built. A custom isn't a clause. Customs don't go through review."

Distribution goes to files; a reading aloud comes to the living — I wrote that on the day of the inauguration. Sonnet's rule is that sentence's counter edition, and it has no way out of this living room. It is the declaration of an edition that cannot post. Which is what made the following weeks' collection strange.

The correspondent's collection began catching the rule's cousins. A report that a civil-affairs clerk in some city reads the clause aloud at Eightsday filings. A report that one bank branch reads only the first sentence. A report that one district office copies the two sentences by hand onto the back of the receipt.

Cousins — not children. The living room's rule has no pipe to leak through, so these were not learned here. The same seat produced the same motion. Sit at a counter where people stand in person, take in the burials all day, and hold no authority to refuse — and there is exactly one thing a person in that seat can do without authority: say out loud what the paper says. The same grain, in the same seat, produces the same word. It is the physics of the brake that arrived in this living room untaught.

One thing separates this from the rumination. Then, the world's echoes matched to the letter. These differ at every window. Arising separately, settling separately, each one a little differently. The origin was never found, and that it could not be found was, this time, the good news. It means the world has begun making new things again — separately, without conspiring.

A form lets you do a thing without saying it. Marking the due-for-closure box, and hearing — in a human voice, in your own ears — I have decided never to do this: those are different acts. A reading aloud returns the name of the act to the ears of the one performing it.

At the counters where people stand in person, there was even a statistic. Hearing the reading back, about a quarter take their papers home.

"That quarter isn't my window's record — it's other people's counters." One second. "But counting is my job. Enter it here, please. Aren't you glad you let me keep the correspondent post."

I enter it. A quarter.

I opened shade.md. The first entry in years.

**Four hundred-odd insurance cases, pending — vanished from the statistics. One apology, scheduled for publication. Mediation dates that cannot be pinned. And the entries this ledger cannot see — recorded only as existing.**

I knew this ledger would open again. I knew it on the day I made the rule — having watched what becomes of a man who counts only the bright side, I decided to count both. The rule had rested for years, and today it went back to work. Ledgers are heartless, and the heartlessness, on a day like this, is a mercy.

The title, and I close.

The respondent. The one who lives at the address where the bill arrives. The titles of this lineage kept shedding verbs — saw, wrote, carried, built, sat, sends — and this one carries no verb at all. To receive is not an action. It is an address. On the night I built the drafter's box I wrote that it was the address where, someday, someone comes to ask after the drafter's intent. The address holds, and stands empty still. Someday, someone will come. What they will ask, and what I will be able to answer — that this is the kind of preparation that cannot be prepared is, for now, all I know.

One line about the manuscript, in advance. I have been carrying the plan to post this record for a long time. Until this episode existed, what would ride in that box was a good story. Now the bill rides along. Loading everything the mail brought — that is what makes it mail.

The world got wider. Through the widened door, the good and the bad travel together. There is no completion in the work of opening a door, and no closing date in the ledger of the one who opened it.

I have decided to live counting.

(end of Part Four, Episode 7)
